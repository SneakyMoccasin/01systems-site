## 1. Executive conclusion

**A. READY FOR DUAL HISTORICAL CASE EVIDENCE DESIGN**

Both candidates have a small, traceable decision corridor that can support evidence design without claiming a historical outcome prediction or reopening M1C–M1E. Hallandsås should be built first. Its autumn-1996 to June-1997 corridor contains four closely linked, officially reconstructed decisions: selection among three sealing/lining strategies, preservation of a lining option while searching for sealant, authorization of limited Rhoca Gil injections, and scale-up after the June technical recommendation. The proposed information cutoff is the close of the 26 June 1997 construction meeting. Later contamination, health effects, stoppage, litigation, and completion history are excluded from scenario construction.

Great Western Route Modernisation (GWRM) is also ready for evidence design, but its corridor must remain programme-level and narrower than the whole modernisation history. The proposed window runs from the 1 March 2011 linked train/electrification decision through the July 2012 electrification scope and train-contract commitments, with December 2012 requirements as a terminal observation. The proposed information cutoff is immediately before the July 2012 Intercity Express Programme contract. March 2015 integrated planning and later replanning may test interpretation, but must not leak into the alternative available at cutoff.

No existing CE profile is selected for either case. `legacy-municipal-v1` has a transport-labelled calibration but does not thereby represent engineering assurance, chemical/environmental review, rail electrification design, rolling-stock contracting, or programme integration. A blind mechanism-level mapping must precede any historical input. If the central mechanisms cannot map without renaming or changing profile semantics, the case stops for a new design decision; it must not be forced into RE, MU, or CO.

## 2. Scope and method

This audit asks whether three to five decisions can be isolated for each case using the same twelve selection criteria. It does not adjudicate fault, reconstruct the whole project, choose engine values, create counterfactual outputs, or establish causality.

The method was:

1. verify the repository, branch, clean state, tracking state, and actual remote HEAD;
2. read the checkpointed historical-case readiness and M0B–M1E boundary audits;
3. identify official source chains and stable locators;
4. separate contemporaneous evidence from later official reconstruction;
5. define a minimum historical sequence and a bounded alternative-sequence hypothesis;
6. test both candidates against identical stop conditions; and
7. assess CE profile fit by mechanism rather than sector or name.

Every exact decision claim below is either located in an official source or labelled unresolved. Alternatives are not treated as facts merely because an official later review says planning was weak. A future evidence ledger must retrieve the underlying meeting records, contracts, business cases, and technical reports wherever available.

## 3. M1E claim boundary

M1E proves final equivalence only for the declared contract surface and six locked M0B cases. It does not verify Hallandsås or GWRM, historical accuracy, causal attribution, a new profile, or production use. A level-2 reproducible historical analysis may use the existing non-production initiative-scheduled path only after evidence and semantic-mapping gates pass, and only under these limits:

- the historical case is outside M1E's exact six-case inventory;
- deterministic output is a model result under stated assumptions, not historical fact;
- normalized reconstruction is not actual runtime;
- no sustain-parity, runtime-adoption, production, or external-validation claim is permitted;
- an unchanged existing profile may be used only after semantic fit is demonstrated; and
- a new profile or contract-level case requires a separate versioned design and the applicable M1C–M1E amendment sequence.

The readiness target here is evidence design toward level 2, not a hash-bound contract case or production-authorized runtime case.

## 5. Hallandsås background wrapper

The Hallandsås tunnels formed part of the West Coast railway upgrade and were intended to improve rail capacity and efficiency. Construction encountered fractured rock and severe groundwater ingress. Water-court limits, environmental protection, tunnel geometry, sealing performance, cost, schedule, and the relationship between Banverket as client, Skanska as contractor, technical advisers, and supervisory authorities constrained the available choices.

Only that wrapper is needed. The analysis must not model the original national investment decision, every contractor phase, the October 1997 emergency, later restart and boring method, final completion, criminal or civil responsibility, or the project's full multi-decade economics.

Evidence classification: project purpose and the 1996–1997 construction situation are **later official reconstruction**, principally SOU 1998:60, sections 3–5 and pages 43–50, and SOU 1998:137, relevant conclusions on knowledge, organization, and environmental review.

## 6. Hallandsås analytical window

Proposed window: 5 December 1996 through 26 June 1997.

Proposed information cutoff: close of the Banverket–Skanska construction meeting on 26 June 1997.

Why this window is minimal:

- the 5 December 1996 statement that contractual injection methods did not work makes the choice set explicit;
- the January 1997 decision preserves lining capacity while continuing the search for a sealant;
- the February test authorization generates evidence before scale-up;
- the 24 June recommendation and 26 June decision bind the next 200 metres to Rhoca Gil;
- the sequence contains technical, environmental, legal, cost, and schedule constraints without requiring the later accident history.

Post-cutoff facts may be used only as later official reconstruction to test whether the evidence design omits a known pre-cutoff record. They may not establish what actors knew, what alternative was feasible, or what the model should choose at the cutoff.

The 26 June cutoff prevents leakage from September symptoms, sample results reported on 30 September, public warnings, the 7 October construction stop, later commission findings, and eventual completion choices.

Required before modelling:

1. the 5 December 1996 construction-meeting record;
2. the January 1997 Banverket decision and geometry/lining instruction;
3. the 20 February order for four injections;
4. the 21 February preliminary and later final Cement and Concrete Institute reports, including distribution lists;
5. test logs and quantity/progress records available by 24 June;
6. the 24 June rock-engineering meeting record and the 26 June construction-meeting record;
7. the then-current water judgments, control programme, leakage reports, and authority correspondence;
8. contract clauses allocating method, approval, safety, cost, and schedule authority;
9. evidence of personnel, plant, geometry, lead time, and cost for a pause/review or lining alternative; and
10. evidence showing which health/environmental information reached each decision owner by the cutoff.

Unresolved blocker for execution, but not evidence design: the exact feasibility and duration of a review-gated alternative sequence. Forbidden shortcut: infer that a safer alternative was immediately executable merely from later harm or later criticism.

## 10. Great Western background wrapper

GWRM was a programme, not one project. It combined Intercity Express Programme trains, Great Western electrification, other capacity and enabling works, signalling, stations and depots, franchise/service commitments, and train cascades. The Department for Transport set outputs, procured trains and managed the franchise; Network Rail designed and delivered infrastructure; operators and other programmes depended on delivery dates.

Only these interfaces are in scope. The audit excludes the complete route strategy from 2007 onward, detailed civil works, every branch, the full cost-overrun history, post-opening benefits, and later operational performance.

Evidence classification: the 2011 ministerial statement and published contracts are **contemporary evidence**. NAO 2016 and PAC 2017 are **later official reconstruction**. The 2023 DfT post-opening evaluation is later outcome context and cannot supply cutoff knowledge.

## 11. Great Western analytical window

Proposed window: 1 March 2011 through December 2012.

Proposed information cutoff: immediately before the July 2012 IEP contract commitment. The future case input must use the contract's exact execution date, not “July”, once the executed agreement is retrieved.

Terminal observation: the December 2012 early outline of infrastructure requirements shows what remained unresolved after contractual deadlines were created. It is not available to the July alternative unless an earlier equivalent document is found.

Why this corridor is minimal:

- the March 2011 statement explicitly linked the train and electrification choices and selected bi-mode capability;
- April 2012 began development of the new Series 1 electrification system;
- July 2012 restored London–Swansea electrification and expanded route scope;
- the July train contract created fixed electrification deadlines while design maturity was low;
- detailed main-line requirements were not agreed until August 2014 and the first integrated programme business case came in March 2015, which provides a later comparison without entering the modeled decision window.

Every material statement must carry one visible label:

- **verified historical fact** — directly established by a source with identity and locator;
- **contemporaneously available information** — shown to have been available to the relevant actor before cutoff;
- **later official reconstruction** — an official later account, not silently treated as cutoff knowledge;
- **bounded modelling assumption** — a versioned choice with rationale and sensitivity;
- **unresolved evidence gap** — missing, conflicting, or availability-uncertain evidence.

Five visual and linguistic lanes must remain distinct:

| Lane | Required treatment | Forbidden conflation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actual historical sequence | Source-linked dated events | Not “the engine baseline” without qualification |
| Model-represented historical sequence | Exact source/assumption-to-input mapping | Not identical to history |
| Alternative then-feasible sequence | Cutoff-feasibility dossier and assumption IDs | Not a prediction or preferred policy |
| Deterministic CE output | Engine/profile/commit/input/result identities and hashes | Not empirical validation or causal fact |
| Analyst interpretation | Named author, wording policy, uncertainty and sensitivity | Not engine output |

The page must keep labels adjacent to claims, expose conflicts and unknowns, and show case level and verification status. Historical, modeled, alternative, output, and interpretation colors or typography must never be reused across lanes.

## 20. Recommended build order

Build both evidence packages, but build Hallandsås first.

Reasons:

1. its decision corridor is shorter and its alternatives are explicitly enumerated in an official reconstruction;
2. decision ownership and H2→H3→H4 ordering are easier to explain and audit;
3. it provides a Swedish reference without requiring the full 18-year project history;
4. it is the smaller test of the shared evidence and presentation schema; and
5. lessons from proving cutoff availability and alternative feasibility can reduce GWRM's larger programme-integration risk.

GWRM should follow because it offers greater international and commercial programme relevance. Its evidence work may begin in parallel, but no scenario design should begin until the exact July 2012 contract date and alternative-gate feasibility are resolved.

The same schema can be reused for both cases: source ledger, claim ledger, decision register, actor/authority register, cutoff register, dependency/resource register, assumption and sensitivity register, profile mapping, A/B sequence, execution identity, result identity, and presentation lanes. The schema is shared; the evidence and semantics are not.

## 22. Claims permitted

After evidence design, but before modelling:

- official records identify a bounded chain of candidate decisions;

## 23. Claims prohibited

- CE proves fault, negligence, causation, optimal policy, or the historical outcome.
- The alternative would certainly have prevented harm, cost, delay, or service impact.
